Got an ADA website complaint about your Shopify store? A plain checklist

This is general information from a company that fixes Shopify themes, not legal advice. Talk to a lawyer who handles ADA website cases.

What's going on

Most US website accessibility lawsuits are filed against online stores, often by the same few plaintiffs' firms. The usual claim is that a blind shopper using a screen reader couldn't complete a purchase. Small businesses commonly settle; accessible.org reports typical website settlements between $5,000 and $20,000, often less for small businesses. Settlements usually come with a deadline to make the site usable.

The checklist

  1. Don't ignore it. A complaint or demand letter has deadlines. Get a lawyer experienced in ADA website cases.
  2. Write down the date and what the complaint names: pages, problems, and any standard it cites (usually WCAG 2.1 AA).
  3. Get a baseline scan of your own store. Free tools such as axe DevTools or Google Lighthouse show code-level failures. They find only part of the problems, but they show where you stand.
  4. Be careful with overlay widgets. They add a toolbar but don't change the code a screen reader reads. The FTC fined one overlay vendor $1 million in 2025 over its claims, and many stores with overlays installed have still been sued.
  5. Fix the theme itself. The common failures in Shopify themes are faint text, images without descriptions, buttons and links without names, unlabeled form fields, and menus that don't work with a keyboard. They are fixed in the theme's Liquid, CSS and JavaScript.
  6. Test with a keyboard. Tab from the header to checkout. If you can't see where you are or you get stuck in a pop-up, a keyboard or screen reader user can't buy.
  7. Keep records. A change log and before/after reports help your lawyer show what was done and when.
  8. Plan for upkeep. New apps, products and theme updates bring problems back. Re-check monthly.
